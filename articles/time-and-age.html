Why does time seem to move faster as we get older?

Our sense of time is not a clock. It is shaped by memory, attention and the way the brain records experience.

Time is more than a clock

Have you ever noticed that childhood summers seemed endless, while entire years can seem to disappear as an adult? This experience is common, but time itself has not changed. Our perception of it has.

The role of new experiences

When we are young, many experiences are new. The brain is constantly learning and creating memories. As routines become familiar, fewer moments may stand out when we look back.

Memory shapes our perspective

Our memories help determine how long a period seems in retrospect. A month filled with new places, people and activities may feel longer in memory than a month spent repeating the same routine.

Can we make time feel richer?

Trying new activities, exploring unfamiliar places and paying closer attention to everyday moments can create more distinctive memories. These experiences may make life feel richer when we look back.

The bigger picture

There is no single explanation for why time seems to accelerate with age. Memory, attention, emotion and routine all play a role. Our experience of time is deeply connected to how we experience life.
